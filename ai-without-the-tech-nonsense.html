Simple Guide

AI Without The Tech Nonsense

A simple guide to understanding and using AI without feeling lost.

Written for adults who are curious, cautious, and tired of being made to feel as though they should have already worked it all out.

What Should I Use First?

If you are new to AI, the first question is not usually “How does it work?” It is “Which one should I use, and which ones can I leave alone for now?”

The simplest answer is this: start with ChatGPT. It is the easiest place to learn the basics because it is built for asking questions, getting plain answers, and trying simple tasks like rewriting an email, summarising a message, or helping you think through a choice.

Once you understand the rhythm of one tool, the others make more sense. They are not magical different worlds. They are different versions of a similar idea, each with its own strengths. You do not need all of them on day one.

Start with ChatGPT if you want

  • A friendly first place to learn.
  • Help with writing, summarising, planning, and simple thinking.
  • Clear conversations where you can keep asking follow-up questions.
  • The easiest route for a beginner who wants to practice without pressure.

Leave the others for later if you want less noise

  • Claude is often liked for long writing, careful reading, and thoughtful text work.
  • Gemini is built into Google tools and can be handy if you already live in Gmail, Google Docs, or Search.
  • Grok tends to feel faster and more conversational, but it is not where most beginners should start.
  • Perplexity is especially useful when you want web-style answers and links, but it feels more like a research tool than a gentle first teacher.

Why ChatGPT first?

Because the goal is not to find the smartest tool. The goal is to find the clearest starting point. ChatGPT is usually the least intimidating place to practise the basics. It helps you learn how to ask, how to read the answer, and how to improve the result without making the experience feel complicated.

If you can have a simple, useful conversation with one AI tool, you will understand the others much faster.

What ChatGPT is best for

  • Writing and rewriting emails.
  • Summarising long text.
  • Helping you think through a decision.
  • Making rough notes clearer.
  • Starting a task when you feel stuck.

What it is not best for

  • Anything private or sensitive.
  • Anything where a wrong answer would matter a lot.
  • Anything that needs sources you have not checked.
  • Anything you would normally ask a professional about instead of a tool.

What the other tools are good at

Think of them as different assistants with slightly different habits. You do not need all of them to begin.

  • Claude: Often strong for long documents, careful reading, and writing that needs a more measured feel.
  • Gemini: Often convenient if you already use Google products and want AI near Gmail, Docs, or Search.
  • Grok: Often used for quick, lively responses and current conversation, but it is not the gentlest place to begin.
  • Perplexity: Often useful when you want answers with links and sources attached so you can check where the information came from.

How to download it

  • ChatGPT: Search for the official ChatGPT app in your app store, or use the website in your browser. If you are unsure, search the name carefully and choose the official OpenAI result.
  • Claude: Search for Claude from Anthropic or use the official website.
  • Gemini: Search for Gemini from Google or use it through Google products where it appears.
  • Grok: Usually available through the X platform or the official Grok experience.
  • Perplexity: Use the official website or app and look for the version that gives answer-plus-links style results.

If you only want one place to begin, skip the comparison game for now and start with ChatGPT. Learn the basics there. The others will feel much easier after that.

Your First Quick Win

The fastest way to feel the value of AI is to use it on something ordinary and annoying. A good first win is turning a messy message into something clear, polite, and easy to send.

This matters because the benefit should be obvious straight away. If the result is noticeably better than what you would have written by yourself, you will feel the point of the tool in your hands instead of only hearing about it in theory.

What to do

  1. Open ChatGPT. This is the easiest place to start.
  2. Paste a rough message. An email, note, or message you need to send.
  3. Ask for a better version. Say you want it clearer, calmer, shorter, or more polite.
  4. Read the result. Keep what works and edit anything that does not sound like you.
  5. Send the final version. You are using AI as a helper, not handing over the job.

What to ask for

  • Clearer wording.
  • A warmer tone.
  • Shorter length.
  • Simple bullet points.
  • A more polite version.
  • A summary of the main points.

Example: turning a rough message into a good one

Input
Please rewrite this so it sounds clear and polite: Hello, I am writing because I am confused about the appointment time and whether I need to bring anything. Also I may be 10 minutes late because of traffic. Please let me know.
Output
Hello, I am writing to confirm the appointment time and whether I need to bring anything with me. I may be around 10 minutes late because of traffic, but I will do my best to arrive on time. Please let me know. Thank you.

Why this counts as a real win

It saves effort. It lowers friction. It gives you something you can use immediately. And it quietly teaches you the basic pattern of AI use: ask clearly, read the answer, improve it, and trust your own judgement at the end.

If you can do this once, you have already done more than most people who only talk about AI without using it.

One safety reminder

Do not paste private or sensitive details into the example. If the real message contains something personal, remove it first and keep the practice version simple.

Quick win 2: shorten something long

Paste a long message, article, or update and ask for the three main points. This is useful when you want the gist without having to read every line.

Paste your message here
[Paste the message, article, or email here] Summarise this into three bullet points and tell me if there is anything I need to do next.

Quick win 3: compare two choices

Paste two options and ask for the simple pros and cons. This is useful for shopping, deciding between services, or working out which choice makes more sense.

Paste your two options here
[Paste option one here] [Paste option two here] Compare these two options in plain English and tell me which one is better value for ordinary use.

What AI Actually Is

AI can sound big and mysterious. In everyday life, it is better to think of it as a tool that has read and seen a huge amount of information, and can respond very quickly when you ask it to help.

It is a bit like having an assistant who is fast, useful, and always available, but not always right. It can help you draft, sort, compare, explain, and summarise. What it cannot do is replace your judgement.

That is the most important idea in this whole guide: AI can be helpful without being reliable in every situation. It is best treated as a starting helper, not as the final authority. Once you understand that, a lot of the confusion lifts.

A useful comparison

Think of AI as a very fast helper, not a wise expert.

Another way to see it

It is like a calculator for words and ideas. It can be handy, but you still need to check the answer.

The simplest truth

AI is not magic. It is a tool. Some tools are useful. Some are used badly. That part still depends on the person holding it.

What makes AI feel strange

Most tools show you clearly what they are doing. AI does not always feel that way. You ask it something, and back comes a smooth answer in full sentences. That can make it feel more certain and more knowing than it really is.

The easiest way to stay grounded is to remember this: AI is very good at producing answers. That is not the same thing as being right.

Think of it like this

AI is useful in the same way a quick assistant is useful. It can save time, tidy things up, and help you get started.

But also like this

AI is risky in the same way a confident stranger can be risky. If the details matter, you still need to check what you were told.

It is not you.

They also tend to skip over the part people actually need most: how this fits into everyday life. You do not need a lecture. You need orientation. You need to know what it is, where it helps, where it does not, and how to use it without feeling exposed.

Your First Simple AI Task

Let’s start with something ordinary: turning a long, wordy email into a short clear one.

This is a good first task because it is useful, easy to judge, and low risk. You are not asking AI to advise you on your health, your money, or anything serious. You are simply asking it to help with wording, which makes it a sensible place to begin.

  1. Open ChatGPT. You can use the website or app.
  2. Paste your rough message. Keep out any private details.
  3. Ask clearly for what you want. Shorter, warmer, clearer, or more polite.
  4. Read the result. Keep what helps. Change what does not sound like you.
  5. Send only when you are happy. You are still the final editor.

Example

Input
Please rewrite this email so it sounds clear and polite: Hello, I am writing because I am confused about the appointment time and whether I need to bring anything. Also I may be 10 minutes late because of traffic. Please let me know.
Output
Hello, I’m just checking the appointment time and whether there is anything I need to bring with me. I may be around 10 minutes late because of traffic, but I will do my best to arrive on time. Please let me know. Thank you.

What this first task teaches you

It shows you that using AI does not have to be dramatic. You ask for help with a specific job. You read the answer. You improve it if needed. You keep your own voice and judgement. That is a strong habit to build from the start.

Another safe first task

Input
Please turn this long message into three bullet points and tell me what I need to do next.
Why this helps
This works well for long emails, appointment details, service updates, travel information, or anything that feels tiring to read when you are already busy.

That is the basic rhythm of using AI well: ask clearly, read carefully, keep your judgement switched on.

How To Get Useful Answers

Better answers usually come from better instructions, not from being more technical.

A lot of people think they need special prompt formulas. They usually do not. What helps most is being clear about what you want, what the answer is for, and how simple you want it to be. The easiest way to begin is to paste your own text first, then ask AI to shape it.

The basic formula

If you are not sure what to type, use this shape:

[Paste the message or text here]
What do you want AI to do with it?
What tone should it use?
How short should it be?

That is enough for most beginner tasks. You do not need clever wording. You need clarity.

The small shift that improves most answers

Instead of saying "help me with this," say what kind of help you want. Shorter. Kinder. More organised. Easier to read. Less formal. More practical. That little extra direction often makes a big difference.

Useful prompt examples

  • For an email: Paste your rough email here. Rewrite this email so it sounds clear and polite.
  • For reading: Paste the article or message here. Summarise it in five simple bullet points.
  • For planning: Paste your plan or idea here. Help me plan a simple day trip with a budget of £100.
  • For comparing: Paste both options here. Compare these two options in plain English and list the main pros and cons.
  • For a decision: Paste the situation here. Help me think through this decision and show me what I may be missing.
  • For explanation: Paste the topic here. Explain this in plain English as if I am completely new to it.
  • For simplification: Paste the text here. Make this shorter, clearer, and easier to understand.

What the answer should feel like

A good answer should feel like it has taken a messy thought and made it easier to use. It should not feel clever for the sake of being clever. It should feel clear, useful, and ready for you to edit.

If the answer is too long, ask for it shorter. If it sounds too formal, ask for warmer language. If it still feels muddy, ask it to try again in simpler terms.

Three things to remember

  • Paste your own text first.
  • Say the result you want clearly.
  • Treat the first answer as a draft, not a verdict.

When AI Is Useful vs When It’s Not

AI is most helpful when the job is about sorting, shaping, or simplifying information.

Useful for

  • Summarising long text.
  • Rewriting emails.
  • Planning simple tasks.
  • Comparing basic options.
  • Explaining unfamiliar topics in plain English.

Not the right tool for

  • Medical advice you would act on without checking.
  • Legal or financial decisions on its word alone.
  • Private, sensitive, or confidential matters.
  • Anything where a wrong answer could cause real harm.

When NOT To Trust AI

AI can sound very sure of itself, even when it is wrong.

  • Do not trust it blindly on facts. Dates, names, prices, and details can be wrong.
  • Do not trust it when the answer seems too neat. A smooth answer is not the same as a correct one.
  • Do not trust it with big decisions. Use it to think, not to decide for you.
  • Do not trust it when it gives no source. If it matters, check it elsewhere.
  • Do not trust it just because it sounds clever. Good wording can hide weak information.

A helpful rule: let AI help you start, sort, or simplify. Do not let it take over your judgement.

What You Should NEVER Type Into AI

This matters. If something is private, personal, or could cause harm if shared, keep it out.

Do not type

  • Bank details.
  • Passwords.
  • Passport or driving licence numbers.
  • Full home address.
  • Private family information.
  • Medical records.
  • Anything confidential from work.

Safer approach

  • Remove names and identifying details.
  • Use made-up examples if you just want help with wording.
  • Keep sensitive information out altogether when possible.

How To Check AI Answers

You do not need a long process. A few simple checks are enough most of the time.

  • Does this sound sensible? Trust your first reaction.
  • Does it answer the question I actually asked? AI sometimes wanders.
  • Would it matter if this were wrong? The bigger the risk, the more you should check.
  • Can I confirm this somewhere reliable? Especially for facts, prices, rules, health, and money.
  • Does it sound too certain? Overconfidence is a warning sign.
  • Would I be comfortable acting on this? If not, pause.

How To Spot AI-Made or Fake Content

Not everything online is fake. But not everything is real either. A few signs can help you slow down before believing or sharing something.

Watch for strange details in images

Odd fingers, warped text, strange reflections, blurry edges, or faces that look almost right but slightly off.

Watch for emotional pressure

If a post tries hard to shock, outrage, or rush you, be cautious.

Check whether other trusted sources show the same thing

If a dramatic claim appears nowhere else, pause before accepting it.

Be wary of perfect certainty

Fake content often sounds as though there can be no doubt at all.

You do not need to become a detective. The aim is simply to stop, look twice, and not hand your trust over too quickly.
